Major Revisions

Provide the pre-registration record. The manuscript's primary defense against the post-hoc hypothesis accusation is its pre-specification of the Neuroticism secondary predictor and the dual-GT analysis strategy. Without a verifiable pre-registration identifier, this defense is rhetorical rather than structural. The registration record should be cited in the Methods section with the registration date clearly predating data collection. If the study was not formally pre-registered, this must be disclosed explicitly and the implications for the Neuroticism finding discussed more conservatively.
Describe the radiologist panel in full. A structured table reporting each of the three independent radiologists' years of experience, subspecialty training, primary institutional affiliation, and any prior involvement with the OAI dataset or the study AI model is required to evaluate the validity of the Platinum Standard. The fourth radiologist facilitator should likewise be characterized. The procedural mechanism by which radiologists were blinded to OAI labels — beyond a verbal statement — should be described (e.g., label fields masked in the annotation platform, platform access logs).
Address the analytical vulnerability of including AI correctness as a covariate. In the M3 GEE model under the Platinum Standard, AI correctness is defined relative to the same Platinum Standard used to define correct annotation — the dependent variable. The authors should demonstrate that the Neuroticism coefficient is substantively stable when AI correctness is removed from the model (M2 vs. M3 comparison appears partially to address this, but the specific comparison of OR = 1.171 in M2 vs. 1.178 in M3 is reassuring and should be called out explicitly as a robustness check rather than buried in a table).
Expand the equity and generalizability analysis. Given the manuscript's central argument that noisy reference standards produce directionally biased evaluations, and given the established literature linking OAI-type data to underdiagnosis bias in marginalized subpopulations (Seyyed-Kalantari et al., 2021), a substantive discussion of who is harmed by the suppressor mechanism — not just which statistical associations are masked — would substantially elevate the clinical relevance of the paper. This does not require new data; it requires a more developed theoretical treatment in the Discussion.
Reframe or relocate the brittle benefit finding. The non-significant Condition × Session interaction (p = 0.554) prevents the scaffold-dependency interpretation from being distinguished from general temporal fatigue. As currently written, Section 4.5 and §5.4 present this finding with a level of narrative emphasis disproportionate to its evidential support. The authors should either reframe this as a hypothesis-generating observation warranting a dedicated future study or provide a more explicit quantitative bound on how much of the Group 1 decline can be attributed to AI withdrawal versus temporal decline.
Replace the ICAR-6 with an age-appropriate instrument in future studies, and add a prospective sample size calculation. The current manuscript correctly identifies the ICAR-6 floor effect as a preventable instrumentation error. For a revision, the authors should provide an a priori power calculation that was conducted for the study (or disclose its absence), and should calculate the sample size required to adequately power the Neuroticism analysis (given OR ≈ 1.18) for future confirmatory work. This calculation — placed in the Discussion or a dedicated power appendix — would convert the manuscript's limitation into a concrete roadmap and strengthen the hypothesis-generating framing.

Minor Revisions

Add a CONSORT-compliant participant flow diagram that explicitly diagrams the 68 enrolled → 51 completed → 17 excluded pathway with reason-level documentation at the participant level rather than the aggregate.
Report confidence intervals for all reliance taxonomy estimates in Table 3. The point estimates for over-reliance (15.6% → 7.8%), appropriate skepticism, and unwarranted skepticism are presented without uncertainty bounds. Bootstrap 95% CIs should be added given the small number of qualifying over-reliance events per participant.
Clarify Figure 4 (The Accuracy Paradox). The figure caption references a "four-bar grouped chart showing No-AI and AI accuracy under the Original GT (60.4%, 65.3%) and Platinum GT (78.4%, 80.8%)," but the actual rendered figure in the manuscript shows a two-bar comparison of "AI Model Content" and "Human Annotator Performance" with orange and blue bars that do not obviously correspond to the caption's description. This figure requires either redesign or caption correction before publication.
Explicitly state the FDR-corrected p-value for Neuroticism (p_FDR = 0.029) in the abstract alongside the uncorrected value. The abstract currently states only "OR = 1.178, 95% CI [1.017, 1.366], p = 0.029" without indicating FDR correction, which may give an inflated sense of confidence to readers who do not reach Table 4's footnote.
Table 4 formatting: The header row distinguishes Platinum GT and Original GT columns but uses inconsistent column label ordering between the table body and the text description. A symmetric layout (Platinum GT | Original GT for each predictor, in the same column order throughout) would improve readability and reduce the risk of column-swapping errors during typesetting.
Terminology standardization: The manuscript alternates between "appropriate reliance," "appropriate AI reliance," and "calibrated reliance" in different sections. A single term should be defined in the Methods and used consistently throughout.
Supplementary materials should be integrated or formally cited. Supplementary Tables S1 and S2 are referenced multiple times but never formally described in the main text. JAMIA requires that supplementary materials be described with sufficient detail in the main manuscript to assess their content without separate access.
