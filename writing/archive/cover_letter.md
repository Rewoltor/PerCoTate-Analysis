Please find enclosed our manuscript entitled, **"Ground Truth Bias in AI-Assisted Medical Annotation: A Crossover Study Using an Independent Radiologist Reference Standard,"** which we are submitting for exclusive consideration as a *Research and Applications* article in JAMIA.

As artificial intelligence is increasingly deployed to support non-expert annotators in clinical labeling pipelines, behavioral evaluations of these human-AI interactions have become critical. However, these evaluations typically rely on public dataset labels as the undisputed ground truth. Our crossover study involving 51 annotators demonstrates a critical methodological vulnerability: when an AI model and the testing labels share a false-negative bias, traditional accuracy metrics create an "accuracy paradox" that artificially inflates the apparent benefit of AI assistance and completely hides clinically dangerous false-negative over-reliance.

By contrasting behavioral outcomes under the original Osteoarthritis Initiative (OAI) labels against an independently constructed radiologist consensus standard, we demonstrate that a highly significant AI-assistance effect can be a measurement artifact. Furthermore, we show how this ground truth suppressor mechanism can obscure genuine psychological predictors of human-AI collaboration, such as the relationship between Neuroticism and appropriate skepticism.

We believe this work is highly relevant to JAMIA’s readership as it directly challenges current methodologies for evaluating AI-assisted diagnostic tools and proposes minimum reporting standards for reference-standard validation and error directionality.
